1. RESPONSE TO REVIEWER 3 — COMMENT 12

We thank the reviewer for emphasizing the need to distinguish the contribution of the contact-map visual representation from that of contrastive regularization. We agree that the original progressive component-wise ablation on DAVIS shows the incremental effect of adding components in the evaluated sequence but does not include a graph-only configuration trained with a contrastive objective.

To address the requested control, we additionally evaluated a Graph-only + InfoNCE configuration on KIBA, the dataset on which PVgraphDTA shows a performance improvement in our benchmark results. This control retains the ligand and protein graph encoders while removing the contact-map visual branch and AE gating. A contrastive objective is applied to the ligand and protein graph representations. Since the full PVgraphDTA contrastive objective also contains terms involving image-based representations, the graph-only control uses an adapted graph–graph contrastive objective rather than the identical multimodal loss.

The Graph-only + InfoNCE control achieved an MSE of 0.142 and a CI of 0.874 on KIBA. The full PVgraphDTA model achieved an MSE of 0.130 and a CI of 0.898 under the reported KIBA evaluation. Hence, the full configuration yielded lower prediction error and higher concordance than the evaluated graph-only contrastive control. This comparison shows that adding the evaluated graph-based contrastive objective to the graph-only scaffold did not reproduce the complete model's KIBA performance.

We also clarify that the autoencoder is pretrained separately to obtain the latent representation used for visual-feature gating. Its reconstruction loss is not jointly optimized with the affinity-prediction and contrastive objectives during PVgraphDTA training.

In response to the reviewer's concern, we have added the KIBA control and revised the discussion to distinguish the DAVIS component-wise results from the additional KIBA comparison. The new result supports a performance difference between the complete multimodal configuration and the evaluated graph-only contrastive control, but it does not independently isolate the contribution of contact-map images: the full model also includes AE gating and an image-dependent multimodal contrastive objective. We therefore avoid attributing the full KIBA improvement exclusively to the visual branch.

4. MANUSCRIPT CHANGE C — CLARIFY THE EXISTING DAVIS TABLE 12 DISCUSSION

LOCATION: Append to the end of the paragraph interpreting the existing component-wise ablation on DAVIS (Table 12), or replace any sentence that attributes the KIBA improvement exclusively to the visual branch.

PASTE:

The DAVIS component-wise ablation evaluates incremental additions of the visual branch, AE gating, and InfoNCE in a fixed sequence. Although AE gating and InfoNCE improved performance relative to their immediately preceding multimodal variants, the graph-only scaffold achieved a lower MSE and higher CI than the complete PVgraphDTA configuration in this DAVIS experiment (0.2018 versus 0.2070 for MSE; 0.9034 versus 0.8750 for CI). The supplementary KIBA control addresses a different question: the complete model achieved better MSE and CI than a graph-only model trained with an adapted graph-based contrastive objective. The effects therefore differ across the evaluated settings, and the KIBA result should not be attributed exclusively to the contact-map visual branch on the basis of these comparisons alone.
