RESPONSE TO REVIEWER AND MANUSCRIPT ADDITIONS (PARAGRAPHS ONLY)

1. RESPONSE TO REVIEWER

We thank the reviewer for highlighting that the functional-group vocabulary-size ablation was not sufficiently explained. We have clarified the composition of the K = 10 and K = 30 vocabularies and the rationale for their selection.

The K = 10 vocabulary comprises carboxylic acid, ester, amide, ketone, alcohol, phenol, ether, primary amine, secondary amine, and aryl. These groups were selected to represent common acidic, carbonyl-containing, hydrogen-bonding, basic, and aromatic chemical functionalities. The K = 20 configuration corresponds to the original twenty functional groups defined in the manuscript.

For K = 30, the original twenty-group vocabulary was extended with ten additional motifs: pyridine, pyrimidine, imidazole, quinazoline, sulfonamide, aryl fluoride, aryl chloride, trifluoromethyl, urea, and acrylamide. These additions were selected to capture nitrogen-containing heterocycles, aromatic halogen substituents, polar nitrogen- and sulfur-containing groups, and an electrophilic amide motif, which are relevant to medicinal chemistry and kinase inhibitor design. We have provided the names and SMARTS definitions of these groups in the revised description of the vocabulary.

We selected K = 30 rather than the previously suggested K = 40 because of available computational and processing limitations and the additional preprocessing and model-evaluation workload associated with a larger vocabulary. We acknowledge that this limits the scope of the vocabulary-size comparison to the configurations considered here, and we have clarified this choice in the revised manuscript.

2. WHAT TO ADD TO THE MANUSCRIPT

A. METHODS — FGgraphDTA / FUNCTIONAL-GROUP FEATURE EXTRACTION

Location: Immediately after the existing table defining the original twenty functional groups, or at the end of the subsection explaining vocabulary construction.

To investigate the effect of functional-group vocabulary size, we considered reduced and expanded representations around the original twenty-group FGgraphDTA vocabulary. The K = 10 subset consists of carboxylic acid, ester, amide, ketone, alcohol, phenol, ether, primary amine, secondary amine, and aryl, selected to retain common acidic, polar, hydrogen-bonding, basic, and aromatic motifs. K = 20 corresponds to the original twenty groups defined above. The expanded K = 30 vocabulary retains those twenty groups and additionally includes pyridine, pyrimidine, imidazole, quinazoline, sulfonamide, aryl fluoride, aryl chloride, trifluoromethyl, urea, and acrylamide to cover further heteroaromatic, halogenated, polar, and electrophilic motifs.

B. RESULTS — FUNCTIONAL-GROUP VOCABULARY-SIZE ABLATION

Location: Immediately after the existing K = 0, 10, 20, and 30 ablation-results table.

We limited the expanded vocabulary to K = 30 rather than K = 40 because of available computational and processing resources. Accordingly, the vocabulary-size analysis compares only the configurations evaluated in this study; it does not establish an optimal vocabulary size for all possible functional-group sets.

3. ADDITIONAL SMARTS DEFINITIONS (PROSE, NOT A TABLE)

The ten additional K = 30 patterns are pyridine (`[n]1ccccc1`), pyrimidine (`[n]1c[n]ccc1`), imidazole (`[nH,n]1c[nH,n]cc1`), quinazoline (`c1ccc2ncncc2c1`), sulfonamide (`[SX4](=[OX1])(=[OX1])[NX3]`), aryl fluoride (`[a]-[F]`), aryl chloride (`[a]-[Cl]`), trifluoromethyl (`[CX4]([F])([F])[F]`), urea (`[NX3][CX3](=[OX1])[NX3]`), and acrylamide (`[CH2]=[CH][CX3](=[OX1])[NX3]`). The K = 10 subset uses the corresponding SMARTS definitions from the original twenty-group table.
