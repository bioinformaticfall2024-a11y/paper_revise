RESULT
The reviewer's central redundancy point is correct, but the recovered implementation applies SMARTS to isolated free-amino-acid SMILES including the common backbone. With RDKit 2025.09.4, the exact code gives 12/20 constant-zero columns (60%), only 8 distinct vectors among 20 residues, maximum categorical capacity log2(8)=3.0 bits, and empirical Shannon entropy 2.219 bits under uniform residue weighting. A,D,E,G,H,I,L,K,S,T,V share one vector; N/Q share one; F/W share one. The vector is deterministic from residue identity and therefore adds no independent information beyond complete one-hot identity.

RESPONSE TO REVIEWER
We thank the reviewer for motivating a direct audit of the protein-side functional-group encoding. We agree with the central point that, because the FG vector is deterministically generated from residue identity, it does not add independent information beyond a complete residue-identity encoding. We reran the exact implementation used in our recovered preprocessing code with RDKit 2025.09.4. This audit showed 12 of the 20 columns were constant zero across the 20 standard residues and the residues produced only 8 distinct FG vectors. The difference from the reviewer's 11-zero/10-vector result arises because our implementation applies the SMARTS patterns to isolated free-amino-acid SMILES including the common backbone rather than side chains alone. We have added these measured coverage/discriminability results and revised the manuscript so that the protein-side FG vector is described as a deterministic engineered re-encoding/inductive bias rather than an additional source of residue information. We also explicitly acknowledge its limited discriminability.

IF RETAINING THE BRANCH
Although deterministic recodings cannot increase information content, they can alter representation geometry and optimization bias by grouping residues according to selected chemical patterns. We therefore retain the protein-side branch only as an engineered inductive-bias experiment, not as a claim of new information; its effect is reported separately and interpreted cautiously.

MANUSCRIPT INSERT
A quantitative audit of the protein-side FG20 encoding showed that it is a deterministic re-encoding of residue identity rather than an independent information source. Under the implemented free-amino-acid SMARTS procedure, 12 of 20 FG dimensions were constant zero across the 20 standard residues and only 8 distinct FG vectors were produced. Accordingly, the protein FG vector should be interpreted as an engineered chemical grouping/inductive bias with limited discriminability. Future work will replace or complement it with context-dependent residue descriptors such as solvent accessibility, local electrostatics, hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor state, pKa-related context and metal-coordination features.
