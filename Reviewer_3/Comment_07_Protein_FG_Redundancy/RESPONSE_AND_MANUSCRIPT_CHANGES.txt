2. REVIEWER RESPONSE — QUANTITATIVE AND INTERPRETATIVE PART (COPY-READY
   ONLY IF THE ORIGINAL TRAINING PREPROCESSING IS CONFIRMED)
=================================================================
We thank the reviewer for the detailed critique of the protein-side functional-group representation. We agree with the central information-theoretic point: because the protein FG20 vector is deterministically derived from amino-acid identity, which is already available to the model through a complete residue one-hot encoding, FG20 cannot supply independent residue-identity information. We therefore no longer describe the protein-side FG20 features as a source of additional independent chemical information.

We performed a quantitative RDKit audit of the 20 SMARTS patterns over all 20 standard amino-acid identities using the free-amino-acid matching and overlap-cleanup procedure in our recovered protein feature implementation. Twelve of the twenty FG20 dimensions were constant zero across these residues (60%), one additional dimension (carboxylic acid) was constant one, and only seven dimensions varied. The twenty residues generated only eight distinct FG20 signatures. Under an assumption of equally frequent amino-acid identities, the resulting FG20 Shannon entropy was 2.219 bits; the maximum capacity of the eight signature categories was log2(8) = 3.0 bits, versus log2(20) = 4.322 bits for twenty distinguishable residue identities. Alanine, aspartate, glutamate, glycine, histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, serine, threonine, and valine shared one signature. Phenylalanine and tryptophan shared a second, and asparagine and glutamine shared a third.

Our counts differ from the reviewer's 11 constant-zero dimensions and 10 unique vectors because the audited encoding applies SMARTS to isolated free amino-acid molecules, including common backbone functional groups, and applies explicit overlap cleanup. Thus, even residues not distinguished by side-chain functional-group patterns have a common nonzero backbone-derived signature; the 20 standard amino acids do not produce literal all-zero FG20 vectors under this encoding. We also verified the audit against chemically correct free-amino-acid structures constructed independently using RDKit; correcting the connectivity errors in the historical amino-acid SMILES dictionary did not alter any of the resulting FG20 binary signatures for these SMARTS patterns. The full per-residue matrix, pattern definitions, and runnable notebook are provided in the revised reproducibility materials.

We agree that the audited degeneracy limits the chemical discriminability of the protein-side FG20 vector. We distinguish this established representational limitation from the hypothesis that the vector caused the observed KIBA CI decrease: the substructure-matching audit alone cannot establish that causal performance mechanism, which would require a matched KIBA experiment with and without protein-side FG20. Likewise, the DAVIS component ablation cannot by itself establish the effect on KIBA.
