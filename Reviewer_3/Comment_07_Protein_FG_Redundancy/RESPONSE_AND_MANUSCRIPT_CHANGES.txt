A. DEFENSIBLE ENGLISH RESPONSE TO REVIEWER (RETENTION ROUTE)
===========================================================================
We thank the reviewer for the detailed critique of the protein-side functional-group representation. We agree with the central information-theoretic observation. The protein FG20 vector is a deterministic function of amino-acid identity, and residue identity is already represented by the one-hot features. Accordingly, the protein FG20 vector cannot provide independent residue-level information. We have revised the manuscript to remove any implication that the protein-side FG20 branch supplies additional independent chemical information.

To quantify its coverage and discriminability, we audited the 20 SMARTS patterns across the 20 standard residue identities using the recovered protein feature-extraction procedure. This procedure applies the patterns to isolated free-amino-acid structures, including the common backbone, with the specified overlap-handling rules. The audit identified 12 constant-zero dimensions (60%), one constant-one dimension, seven varying dimensions, and eight distinct 20-bit signatures. Assuming uniformly weighted amino-acid identities, the signature entropy was 2.219 bits, compared with 4.322 bits for 20 distinguishable identities. For example, histidine shared its FG20 signature with ten other residue types. We therefore acknowledge that protein FG20 has limited chemical discriminability and does not uniquely encode residue identity.

Our count differs from the reviewer's reported 11 constant-zero dimensions and 10 distinct vectors. In the audited whole-free-amino-acid encoding, every residue activates the common carboxylic-acid feature, so none of the seven residues identified by the reviewer has a literally all-zero FG20 vector. The difference in results should be evaluated with reference to whether the matching inputs represent isolated side chains or whole free amino acids and whether identical overlap rules are applied. As an additional check, the FG20 audit gave the same signatures when performed on independently constructed, chemically correct free-amino-acid structures. We provide the executable notebook and per-residue matrix so that these implementation-specific results can be examined and reproduced. We do not dispute the reviewer's broader conclusion that the vector is redundant with residue identity.

We respectfully distinguish informational redundancy from the empirical effect of an engineered feature recoding on a finite learned model. A deterministic chemical grouping cannot increase the information available beyond a complete one-hot encoding, but presenting that grouping explicitly can alter a model's learning dynamics and inductive bias. Consistent with a possible representation effect, the existing same-split DAVIS ablation in Table 11 reports MSE = 0.1648 for ligand FG alone and MSE = 0.1546 for combined ligand and protein FG, a numerical reduction of 0.0102 when the protein-side features are added in that reported experiment. The protein-FG-only configuration also reports MSE = 0.1822 compared with 0.2018 for the graph-only configuration. We emphasize that these single-split observations do not establish a statistically robust gain, an increase in independent chemical information, or the cause of the lower CI observed on KIBA.

We have chosen to retain the original protein FG20 branch in the present study, while explicitly revising its interpretation: it is an auxiliary, deterministic, coarse chemical recoding of residue identity whose contribution in the present experiments is empirical rather than information-theoretic. We now report its measured coverage and limitations and avoid attributing the KIBA ranking behavior to it as an experimentally established mechanism. We acknowledge that retaining the branch does not remove the redundancy identified by the reviewer. A redesign using validated context-dependent residue properties, or a matched study omitting the protein FG branch, remains necessary to determine whether a different representation gives a more reliable benefit across datasets.
