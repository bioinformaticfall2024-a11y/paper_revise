A. FINAL RESPONSE TO REVIEWER — COMMENT 15
============================================================

We thank the reviewer for identifying this ambiguity and prompting this clarification.

1. Main DAVIS benchmark cohort

We clarify that the main FGgraphDTA and PVgraphDTA experiments reported in Table 8 used the complete DAVIS benchmark comprising 68 drugs, 442 target entries, and 30,056 drug–target interactions, using the same nominal DAVIS benchmark population reported in the literature. Complete PconsC4 contact maps and HHblits alignment files were available for all 442 target entries used in the main benchmark workflow. Therefore, the reduction to 287 targets in the additional cold-target experiment was not caused by unavailable PconsC4 contact maps, failed contact-map generation, or missing alignment files.

For the main DAVIS benchmark experiments, the 30,056 interactions were divided using the reported 80/10/10 random interaction split, corresponding to 24,044 training interactions, 3,006 validation interactions, and 3,006 test interactions.

2. Nature of the 287-target cold-target cohort

The 287-target / 19,516-interaction population was specific to the additional protein-disjoint (cold-target) experiment performed during revision and was constructed from a separately prepared, pre-filtered tabular DAVIS dataset. We acknowledge that describing these as "287 usable proteins" in the previous revision was imprecise because it could be interpreted as indicating that the remaining DAVIS targets lacked usable structural information.

We further agree with the reviewer that target identity and sequence redundancy require particular care in DAVIS. A sequence-level audit of the 287-target subset showed that it contains 268 unique sequence strings. The subset retains certain modified target entries, including BRAF(V600E), EGFR(L858R), FLT3(D835H), and KIT(D816V), while omitting some other kinase entries such as ABL2, BTK, and JNK1–3. Thus, the 287-target cohort should not be retrospectively described as a strictly canonical wild-type subset. Likewise, because the complete PconsC4 and alignment resources were available for the main 442-target benchmark, the 155 omitted target entries should not be retrospectively attributed to PconsC4 failure or shallow multiple-sequence alignments.

The historical construction record for this separately prepared 287-target table does not preserve a complete auditable first-failure rule for all 155 omitted target entries. Rather than assigning an unsupported post-hoc biological or structural explanation, we now report the exact cohort composition and sample sizes and explicitly limit the interpretation of the cold-target experiment to this evaluated subset.

3. Cold-target partition and matched comparison

For the additional cold-target experiment, the 287 target entries correspond to 19,516 drug–target interactions (68 × 287). Fifty-eight target entries (20.2%) and all 3,944 associated interactions were held out completely for testing, while the remaining 229 target entries and 15,572 interactions were used for training. No interaction associated with a held-out target identifier was included in the training set. No separate validation subset was reported for this additional cold-target analysis.

Importantly, DGraphDTA and FGgraphDTA were evaluated using the same 287-target cohort and the same cold-target partition, so their comparison within this experiment is matched with respect to the evaluated target population. FGgraphDTA achieved MSE = 0.4252 and CI = 0.8106, compared with MSE = 0.4460 and CI = 0.8020 for DGraphDTA. The paired analysis across the 58 held-out target entries yielded t(57) = 2.13 and p = 0.038.

We have revised the manuscript to clearly distinguish the complete 442-target / 30,056-interaction DAVIS benchmark used in the main experiments from the 287-target / 19,516-interaction subset used only for the additional cold-target experiment. We also revised the wording so that the cold-target cohort is no longer described as "287 usable proteins," and we limit the cold-target generalization claim to the evaluated cohort.
