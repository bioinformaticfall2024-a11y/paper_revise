Background
Predicting the binding affinity between drugs and target proteins is an important step in computer-aided drug discovery. Although deep learning approaches have advanced this task, effectively incorporating chemically relevant features and protein structural information remains challenging.

Results
We present two complementary graph-based methods for drug–target affinity prediction: FGgraphDTA incorporates functional-group information into ligand and protein graphs, while PVgraphDTA uses protein contact maps to capture structural relationships. On DAVIS, FGgraphDTA achieved lower MSE and higher CI and Pearson correlation than the literature-reported DGraphDTA reference, whereas PVgraphDTA did not show comparable gains. On KIBA, PVgraphDTA performed better than FGgraphDTA across all three metrics, but neither model exceeded the published DGraphDTA results. Both proposed models also showed lower Pearson correlations on KIBA (0.873 and 0.877, respectively) than the published DGraphDTA value (0.903).

Conclusions
FGgraphDTA and PVgraphDTA offer complementary ways to incorporate chemical and protein structural information into drug–target affinity prediction. Their performance varied by dataset and model, highlighting the importance of reporting multiple evaluation metrics rather than claiming uniform improvements across benchmarks.
